## Migration plan

### Wave 1 — Eliminate classically broken primitives

These are exploitable today with classical hardware. They are not a quantum problem and should not wait for a quantum programme.

- Artefacts: 6
- Effort: 36 engineer-days
- Targets: SHA-384, TLSv1.3

### Wave 2 — Upgrade cryptographic providers

Libraries, runtimes and container images must expose ML-KEM and ML-DSA before anything above them can use them. This wave unblocks every later wave and is mostly dependency bumps.

- Artefacts: 3
- Effort: 45 engineer-days

### Wave 3 — Raise protocol floors

TLS 1.2 and earlier cannot negotiate a hybrid key-exchange group at all, so the protocol floor has to move before PQC key agreement is even expressible on the wire.

- Artefacts: 9
- Effort: 81 engineer-days
- Targets: AES-256, ML-KEM-768, TLSv1.3, X25519MLKEM768

### Wave 4 — Migrate key agreement (harvest-now-decrypt-later)

Recorded handshakes protected by ECDH, X25519 or finite-field DH are decryptable retroactively the moment a CRQC exists. Confidentiality of data already in flight depends on this wave, which is why it precedes signatures.

- Artefacts: 1
- Effort: 45 engineer-days
- Targets: ML-KEM-768

### Wave 5 — Migrate signatures, certificates and key material

Signature forgery requires a live CRQC rather than stored ciphertext, so this wave has real deadline pressure but no retroactive exposure. Composite certificates allow migration before every verifier is ready.

- Artefacts: 2
- Effort: 60 engineer-days
- Targets: ML-DSA-65

### Wave 6 — Vendor-gated hardware and managed services

HSM firmware and cloud KMS key types are outside your release cycle. Track vendor roadmaps and plan the cutover around them.

- Artefacts: 3
- Effort: 90 engineer-days

### Wave 7 — Strengthen symmetric parameters

Grover halves effective symmetric key length. AES-128 to AES-256 and SHA-256 to SHA-384 are parameter changes with no protocol impact, so they are safe to schedule last.

- Artefacts: 2
- Effort: 3 engineer-days
- Targets: AES-256
